# The Change In Perspective

Before the start of my second semester, I did not know what I would learn in the course "**Becoming A Reflective Information Scientist**". I guessed it would be somewhere on the lines of _sociology_ and _philosophy_, mostly because we were doing similar courses in college even when our actual domains were in the field of sciences. Looking back at it, the course title was quite self-explanatory. 
To become a good information scientist one must not only know how to decipher information themselves but also have the ability to show that information in its most accurate and digestible form. We must be reflective individuals who can understand the data to its most basic forms and then show that information to the world. As Albert Einstein once said:

> If you can't explain it simply, you don't understand it well enough.

I learned a little on how to decipher information and organize it using the **DIKW model**. The DIKW model stands for **Data, Information, Knowledge and Wisdom**. In each step we refine what data we are given into something that has more context and intention. An example of using the DIKW model would be to use it to refine Sleep Patterns. We can collect some raw data like Sleep Duration, Deep Sleep Time, etc. We can then try to contextualize that data by asking some questions like "**What is the Average Sleep Duration?**" etc, being Information. Then we can ask some more personalized questions like "**How can we apply this to improve sleep patterns?**" being Knowledge. Combining this all together we can come up with a solution to the problem by _using all the data, information and knowledge together_. This is Wisdom. 

I also learned about how the human brain conceptualizes data visuals like graphs and histograms. It was very interesting to learn about perspectives of people and how, although we may understand our own graphs, it is hard for people who don't have the context required to understand it as well as us. It is always important to create ways to interpret data that is easy to understand for everyone.
